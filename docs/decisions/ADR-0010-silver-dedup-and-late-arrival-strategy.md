# ADR-0010: Bounded-Watermark Streaming Dedup (AUTO CDC) + Nightly Batch Reconciliation for Late Arrivals

## Status
Accepted

## Context
Silver must deduplicate meter readings keyed on `(meter_id, reading_timestamp)` (meters/gateways can redeliver the same reading on retry — see [ARCHITECTURE.md § Bronze → Silver](../architecture/ARCHITECTURE.md#3-bronze--silver)) while remaining a continuous streaming pipeline for on-time data (Silver latency < 5 minutes, per the platform's non-functional requirements). Structured Streaming's stateful operators (dedup, the MERGE this requires) must bound how much state they retain per key, or state grows unboundedly as the fleet accumulates history — at 10M–100M meters reporting every 15 minutes, unbounded per-key state is not viable. Bounding that state via a watermark necessarily means *some* readings — a meter reconnecting after a multi-day connectivity gap — will arrive after their watermark has already closed, and the streaming path alone cannot land them once state has been discarded for their key.

Options considered:

1. **Lakeflow AUTO CDC MERGE flow** (`dp.create_auto_cdc_flow`, `stored_as_scd_type=1`) keyed on `(meter_id, reading_timestamp)`, fed by a 24-hour-watermarked stream, **plus** a separate nightly batch job that reconciles anything the watermark missed by anti-joining Bronze against Silver
2. **`dropDuplicatesWithinWatermark`** (Structured Streaming's built-in watermark-bounded dedup operator) writing directly to `silver.meter_readings`, no separate reconciliation job — readings arriving after the watermark are simply dropped
3. **No watermark** — unbounded dedup state, so every late arrival is eventually deduplicated correctly by the streaming job itself, at the cost of ever-growing state
4. **Silver as a pure batch (not streaming) pipeline** — process Bronze in a daily/hourly triggered batch, dedup via a plain MERGE over the full accumulated Bronze partition each run, no watermark needed since batch runs aren't bounded by streaming state at all

## Decision
Use **option 1**: a 24-hour-watermarked streaming AUTO CDC MERGE flow for on-time dedup, plus `src/jobs/silver_late_arrival_reconciliation.py` as a nightly batch job that anti-joins Bronze against Silver (by the same `(meter_id, reading_timestamp)` key) to land anything still missing, sharing the exact same enrichment/validation logic (`src/libs/common/enrichment.py`, `src/libs/quality/expectations.py`) as the streaming path so a late-arriving reading is judged identically to an on-time one.

## Rationale

- **vs. `dropDuplicatesWithinWatermark` with no reconciliation (option 2)**: this is the simplest implementation, but it makes "arrived more than 24 hours late" equivalent to "silently discarded," which directly contradicts this platform's zero-data-loss requirement and ARCHITECTURE.md's explicit design ("Readings arriving after the watermark still land in Silver via a nightly batch reconciliation pass — not dropped"). A billing-relevant water reading that a meter finally reports after a week-long connectivity outage is exactly the kind of record this platform cannot afford to lose.
- **vs. unbounded state (option 3)**: removes the "silently dropped" risk without a second job, but streaming state that grows without bound as the fleet's history accumulates is an operational time bomb at this platform's target scale (100M meters) — checkpoint size, recovery time after a restart, and cluster memory pressure all degrade continuously rather than staying flat. A watermark is the standard, supported way to keep a streaming stateful operator's resource footprint bounded and predictable; giving that up to avoid building a second job trades a scaling problem for a convenience.
- **vs. pure batch Silver (option 4)**: satisfies zero-data-loss and avoids a watermark entirely, but abandons the < 5 minute Silver latency requirement for *all* data, not just the late-arriving tail — the vast majority of readings arrive on time and don't need batch-scale latency. This also reopens the two-orchestration-system cost ADR-0003 already rejected Airflow over: a batch-only Silver still needs Lakeflow or an equivalent scheduler, but now has to reimplement what Structured Streaming's watermarking already does well (bounding state for the common case) just to avoid it for the uncommon case.
- **Why AUTO CDC specifically, not a hand-rolled `foreachBatch` MERGE**: `dp.create_auto_cdc_flow` is Lakeflow's declarative primitive for exactly this "streaming source, MERGE-based upsert target, dedup by key + sequence column" shape, and gets pipeline-native lineage, metrics, and Predictive-Optimization-compatible table maintenance for free (ADR-0004) — a hand-rolled `foreachBatch` achieving the same MERGE semantics would need to reimplement idempotent batch-ID tracking and wouldn't get those same operational surfaces automatically. This follows directly from ADR-0003's Lakeflow choice; this ADR only decides the watermark/reconciliation strategy layered on top of it.
- **Why the reconciliation job shares code with the streaming pipeline, not a separate implementation**: a late-arriving reading and an on-time reading must be validated and enriched by the same rules, or "reason a row was quarantined" would depend on *when* it happened to arrive rather than what's actually wrong with it — an inconsistency that would be confusing to debug and could let a business rule silently drift between the two paths over time.

## Consequences

- Silver's dedup guarantee is **eventually** exact, not immediately exact: a reading arriving 1–24 hours after `reading_timestamp` is deduplicated within the streaming SLA; a reading arriving after 24 hours is deduplicated within the next reconciliation run's window (up to ~24 hours after that, given the nightly schedule) — this bounded-but-nonzero reconciliation lag is an accepted trade for bounded streaming state, and must be documented for any downstream consumer (e.g. Gold aggregates, Phase 5) that queries Silver before a given day's reconciliation window has closed.
- Two independent writers exist for `silver.meter_readings` (the streaming AUTO CDC flow and the nightly batch MERGE), which can hit Delta concurrent-write conflicts — mitigated, not eliminated, by scheduling reconciliation during a low-traffic window (03:00 UTC, see `bundles/silver_late_arrival_reconciliation_job.yml`); a genuine conflict fails the batch job cleanly (Delta's optimistic concurrency control) and it is safe to retry, since `_late_arriving_bronze_rows`' anti-join is idempotent.
- The reconciliation job's lookback window (default 7 days, `late_arrival_reconciliation_lookback_days`) is itself a tunable trade-off between reconciliation cost (re-scanning more Bronze history every night) and how late an arrival can be before it's missed entirely — a reading arriving more than the lookback window late still would not be silently dropped (Bronze retains it permanently), but would need a manual/wider one-off reconciliation run, not the nightly default.
